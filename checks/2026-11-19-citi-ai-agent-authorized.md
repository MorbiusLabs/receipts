# Citi: whatever a third-party AI agent does in your account counts as "authorized by You"

**Post:** [X](https://x.com/MorbiusLabs/status/2123500086269186438) · [morbiuslabs.org](https://morbiuslabs.org/p/2026-11-19-citi-ai-agent-authorized/) · **Checked:** 2026-11-19 · **Status:** Citi claims from the agreement itself; other banks via TechCrunch

Citi added a new Section H to its Online User Agreement this month: "Use of Third-Party Artificial Intelligence ("AI") Agents". We read the live text.

| # | Claim | Verdict | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Citi changed its online agreement to cover third-party AI agents | **REAL** | [Citi Online User Agreement](https://www.citi.com/tandc/view-printable-version): "last updated November 2026 to add a new Section H titled Use of Third-Party Artificial Intelligence" agents |
| 2 | If you give an AI agent your login, Citi treats everything it does as yours | **REAL** | Section H: "Citi will consider any access and actions taken by the agent as authorized by You" |
| 3 | Same if the agent leaks your credentials | **REAL** | Section H: if credentials given to the agent "are further shared, leaked, or otherwise distributed to other persons or AI agents", Citi treats actions taken with them "as authorized by You" |
| 4 | Citi will not check agent actions, even ones that go against your instructions | **REAL** | Section H: Citi has "no responsibility or other obligation to investigate" such actions "even if" they "conflict with any prompts or other instructions provided by You". Monitoring the agent is "your responsibility and not Citi's" |
| 5 | Customers now have no legal protection at all | **NOT CLAIMED** | Section H makes you responsible for agent errors "except as otherwise permitted under applicable law". It does not say how that sits with federal rules on unauthorized transfers |
| 6 | The rule also covers Citi's own AI tools | **FALSE** | Section H: "these terms do not apply to your use of any third-party AI Agent, tool, function or feature provided by Citi" |
| 7 | Citi's privacy notice does not protect data you hand to the agent | **REAL** | Section H: "the Citi Privacy Notice does not apply to the third-party AI Agent" |
| 8 | JPMorgan, Bank of America, PNC and Citizens have taken similar steps | **REAL (their reporting)** | [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/18/more-banks-says-customers-using-ai-agents-are-on-their-own-even-if-they-get-hacked/), Nov 18. It quotes no text from those banks; we did not open their terms |
| 9 | US payment rules already treat transfers by someone you gave access to as authorized | **OUR READ** | [Regulation E, 12 CFR 1005.2(m)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/12/1005.2): an unauthorized transfer does not include one "initiated by a person who was furnished the access device to the consumer's account by the consumer", unless you told the bank to stop. Neither Citi nor TechCrunch cites this; how it applies to AI agents is untested |

## What to do

If an AI tool has your Citi login, assume Citi will treat its actions and any leak as yours. Revoke the access in the tool, change the password, and use read-only connections where they exist.
